# Report-writing guidance

## Build from evidence

Use the actual task result, supplied artifacts, tool outputs, and verified sources. Label inference as inference and state key assumptions. If an important fact could not be verified, say so plainly.

## Recommended structure

Adapt these sections to the task:

1. **Summary** — outcome and why it matters.
2. **Scope** — what was examined or changed, including exclusions.
3. **Findings** — facts, evidence, and impact in priority order.
4. **Recommendations / next steps** — concrete actions, owner or priority when known.
5. **Risks and limitations** — uncertainty, gaps, and unverified items.

For a short request, use a compact summary plus findings and next step rather than forcing all headings. For technical changes, include affected files/components and verification performed. For decisions, compare viable options against the user’s criteria.

## Style

Use direct language, precise dates and units, and descriptive links or citations when sources are available. Avoid filler, repeated conclusions, and claims of certainty that the evidence cannot support.
